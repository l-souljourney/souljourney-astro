## **📚 2. Article Structure (three-level headings suggested)**

### **1. Introduction: Life Is Also a Market Trade**

- Open with a question: "Why do some people work harder and get poorer? Why do others grow richer without making any noise?"
- State the core claim:
    > Life is not controlled by fate, but by the "cognitive market".
    > Each of us buys and sells the chips of our own life with expectations.
- Bring in a small story (e.g., young people changing jobs frequently, failed startups, blind investing — these are all "trades on wrong expectations")

💡 **Provocative line:**

> It is not effort that determines the outcome; it is "how you understand yourself" that determines the price at which you trade.

---

### **2. Expectations: The You You Think You Are Determines the Direction You Bet On**

- Corresponds to "market trading expectations"
- Explain: your "expectation" in life is your imagination of your future self.
    - Some overestimate themselves → recklessness and blindness;
    - Some underestimate themselves → giving up and self-limitation;
    - Very few can "price themselves accurately".
- Use the concept of a "psychological market": everyone trades their future, but few know what they are worth.
- Extend to "self-expectation bias" (mispricing of ability, time, and risk)

💬 **Thought sentence:**

> Do you bet on your life with reason, or gamble with emotion?

---

### **3. Buying and Selling: Between the Perception Gap and the Action Gap, Wealth Is Redistributed**

- Corresponds to "buying and selling determine wealth"
- Extend to the metaphor: "perception is valuation, action is execution".
- Emphasize:
    - Those who perceive correctly but do not act remain at "paper gains";
    - Those who act a lot but perceive wrongly suffer "frequent losses".
- Analogies:
    - Emotional investment (misjudging the value of a relationship)
    - Career choice (misreading the trend)
    - Finance / entrepreneurship (impulsive trading)

🔥 **Suggested controversial topics:**

- "You are not short of opportunities; you are short of one 'cognitive stop-loss'."
- "Blind action is scarier than laziness, because it is high-frequency losing."

💬 **Thought sentence:**

> Is your action cashing in your perception, or escaping your anxiety?

---

### **4. Supply and Demand: The Price of Self-Worth Depends on How Much You Are Needed**

- Corresponds to "supply and demand determine price"
- Turn economic logic into the logic of "life positioning":
    - Supply: what can you offer?
    - Demand: what do others truly need?
- Point out the common bias:
    > People output "supply" self-centeredly while ignoring the real "demand" of the market.
    - Like entrepreneurs building products nobody needs;
    - Like employees working overtime on things that do not matter.
- Introduce "the liquidity of self-worth": your price depends on whether you are scarce in your market.

🔥 **Suggested controversial topics:**

- "True value is not how hard you work, but whether you are needed."
- "Effort is not a scarce commodity; solving other people's problems is."

💬 **Thought sentence:**

> If you left this market, would anyone feel the scarcity?

---
